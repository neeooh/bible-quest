# Why Read Each Book of the Bible?

Short introductions to the 66 books of the World English Bible (WEB),
explaining what each book offers the reader.

## Old Testament

### Genesis

Read Genesis to discover the Bible's account of creation, humanity's
beginnings, and the origin of Israel. Its stories explore faith,
failure, family, and God's promises.

### Exodus

Read Exodus to follow Israel's escape from slavery in Egypt and the
beginning of its covenant with God. It introduces themes of freedom,
worship, justice, and God's presence with his people.

### Leviticus

Read Leviticus to understand the laws and rituals that shaped Israel's
worship and daily life. It explores holiness, sacrifice, purity, and
what it means to live in relationship with a holy God.

### Numbers

Read Numbers to follow Israel's difficult journey through the wilderness
toward the Promised Land. Its stories show the consequences of distrust
and rebellion, but also God's patience and faithfulness.

### Deuteronomy

Read Deuteronomy as Moses prepares a new generation of Israelites to
enter the Promised Land. It gathers the law into a call to remember God,
love him, and choose a faithful way of life.

### Joshua

Read Joshua to see how Israel enters and settles in the Promised Land.
The book explores courage, obedience, leadership, and the importance of
remaining faithful to God.

### Judges

Read Judges to understand a turbulent period when Israel repeatedly
turned away from God and suffered the consequences. Its cycles of
failure and rescue show the cost of moral confusion and the need for
faithful leadership.

### Ruth

Read Ruth for a quiet but powerful story of loyalty, kindness, and hope
amid loss. It shows how faithfulness in ordinary decisions can become
part of a much larger story.

### 1 Samuel

Read 1 Samuel to follow Israel's transition from judges to kings through
the lives of Samuel, Saul, and David. It examines leadership, obedience,
ambition, and the difference between outward appearance and the heart.

### 2 Samuel

Read 2 Samuel to explore David's reign, including his achievements,
failures, and painful family conflicts. It shows both the possibilities
and dangers of power, while tracing God's promises to David.

### 1 Kings

Read 1 Kings to discover how Israel's united kingdom divides and begins
to decline. The stories of Solomon and the prophets challenge readers to
consider the consequences of compromise and divided loyalty.

### 2 Kings

Read 2 Kings to follow the final years of Israel and Judah, ending in
conquest and exile. It offers a sobering account of how persistent
injustice and unfaithfulness can destroy a nation.

### 1 Chronicles

Read 1 Chronicles to revisit Israel's history with special attention to
David, worship, and the temple. Its genealogies and stories help connect
the community's identity with its calling and heritage.

### 2 Chronicles

Read 2 Chronicles to explore the kings of Judah and the spiritual life
of the nation. It highlights the consequences of seeking or abandoning
God, and ends with a note of hope for restoration.

### Ezra

Read Ezra to see how Jewish exiles return to Jerusalem and rebuild the
temple. It explores renewal, commitment to Scripture, and the challenges
of restoring a community after disaster.

### Nehemiah

Read Nehemiah to follow the rebuilding of Jerusalem's walls and the
renewal of its people. It offers practical lessons about prayer,
courageous leadership, perseverance, and shared responsibility.

### Esther

Read Esther to discover how a Jewish woman risks her life to protect her
people in the Persian Empire. The story explores courage, identity,
providence, and standing up against injustice.

### Job

Read Job to wrestle with suffering, injustice, and the limits of human
understanding. It challenges simplistic explanations of pain and invites
deeper reflection on faith when life makes little sense.

### Psalms

Read Psalms to find language for the full range of human experience,
from joy and gratitude to fear, grief, and anger. These poems and
prayers teach readers how to bring honest emotions before God.

### Proverbs

Read Proverbs for practical wisdom about character, relationships, work,
speech, money, and decision-making. Its short teachings encourage a life
shaped by humility, discipline, and reverence for God.

### Ecclesiastes

Read Ecclesiastes for an honest examination of pleasure, achievement,
work, wealth, and the search for meaning. It challenges readers to face
life's limits and consider what truly matters.

### Song of Solomon

Read Song of Solomon for poetic reflections on romantic love, desire,
and mutual delight. It celebrates the beauty of committed human
affection through vivid and intimate poetry.

### Isaiah

Read Isaiah for a sweeping vision of judgment, hope, justice, and
restoration. Its powerful poetry calls people away from empty religion
and points toward God's promised salvation.

### Jeremiah

Read Jeremiah to hear the warnings of a prophet who speaks to Judah as
its destruction approaches. His life and message reveal the cost of
truth-telling, the seriousness of injustice, and the hope of a renewed
covenant.

### Lamentations

Read Lamentations to encounter the grief of a people after Jerusalem's
destruction. Its poems give voice to suffering while showing that sorrow
and hope can exist side by side.

### Ezekiel

Read Ezekiel for vivid visions and symbolic actions addressing a people
in exile. The book explores accountability, God's glory, personal
responsibility, and the promise of spiritual and national renewal.

### Daniel

Read Daniel for stories of faithfulness under foreign rule and visions
about the rise and fall of kingdoms. It encourages courage, wisdom, and
trust in God when powerful institutions demand compromise.

### Hosea

Read Hosea through the story of a broken marriage that portrays Israel's
unfaithfulness and God's persistent love. It confronts betrayal and
injustice while offering a moving picture of mercy and restoration.

### Joel

Read Joel to explore a national crisis described through a devastating
locust plague and the call to return to God. It points toward the day of
the Lord and the promise that God's Spirit will be poured out.

### Amos

Read Amos for a forceful challenge to religious hypocrisy and social
injustice. It insists that worship means little when the vulnerable are
exploited and justice is ignored.

### Obadiah

Read Obadiah to understand a short prophetic judgment against Edom for
its pride and hostility toward Judah. It warns that arrogance and taking
advantage of others will not go unanswered.

### Jonah

Read Jonah for a story about a reluctant prophet who resents God's
compassion toward his enemies. It challenges narrow ideas of mercy and
asks whether readers can welcome compassion for people they dislike.

### Micah

Read Micah for a clear call to practise justice, love mercy, and walk
humbly with God. Its messages confront corruption while holding out hope
for a future ruler and a restored people.

### Nahum

Read Nahum to hear the announcement of Nineveh's downfall and the end of
its oppressive power. It offers assurance that cruelty and violence do
not escape God's judgment forever.

### Habakkuk

Read Habakkuk to follow a prophet's honest questions about evil,
injustice, and God's apparent silence. It shows that faith can include
difficult questions and can endure even when circumstances do not
improve.

### Zephaniah

Read Zephaniah for a warning about the day of the Lord and a call to
seek humility and righteousness. Its message moves from judgment toward
the hope of a purified people and renewed joy.

### Haggai

Read Haggai to understand why the returned exiles are urged to rebuild
the temple rather than focus only on their own homes. It challenges
readers to examine their priorities and put lasting commitments ahead of
convenience.

### Zechariah

Read Zechariah for visions and promises that encourage a discouraged
community rebuilding Jerusalem. It combines calls to faithfulness with
hope for God's future kingdom and an ideal king.

### Malachi

Read Malachi for a direct challenge to careless worship, corrupt
leadership, and unfaithfulness. It calls readers to take their
commitments seriously and anticipates a messenger who will prepare the
way for the Lord.

## New Testament

### Matthew

Read Matthew to explore Jesus as the promised Messiah and fulfilment of
Israel's Scriptures. Its teachings, parables, and account of his life
invite readers to understand what life in God's kingdom looks like.

### Mark

Read Mark for a fast-moving account of Jesus' actions, authority,
suffering, and resurrection. It presents discipleship as a demanding
path of trust, service, and following Jesus even when the way is
difficult.

### Luke

Read Luke for a detailed account of Jesus' life that gives particular
attention to outsiders, the poor, women, and the overlooked. It
highlights God's mercy and the wide reach of the good news.

### John

Read John to explore who Jesus is through signs, conversations, and
extended teachings. Its central invitation is to believe in Jesus and
find life through him.

### Acts

Read Acts to follow the early Christian movement from Jerusalem into the
wider Roman world. It shows how the Holy Spirit empowers ordinary people
to build communities and carry the message of Jesus across cultural
boundaries.

### Romans

Read Romans for a systematic exploration of sin, grace, faith,
salvation, and life in the Spirit. It helps readers understand the
foundations of the Christian message and how those beliefs should shape
everyday life.

### 1 Corinthians

Read 1 Corinthians to see how a divided church is challenged over
relationships, worship, morality, and spiritual gifts. It offers
practical guidance for building a community shaped by love rather than
status or rivalry.

### 2 Corinthians

Read 2 Corinthians for a personal account of Christian ministry,
weakness, suffering, and reconciliation. It challenges the idea that
strength comes from status and shows how vulnerability can serve others.

### Galatians

Read Galatians for a passionate defence of freedom through faith in
Christ rather than reliance on religious boundary markers. It explores
grace, identity, and the responsibility to use freedom in love.

### Ephesians

Read Ephesians to explore the unity of believers in Christ and the new
identity they share. It connects big ideas about grace and
reconciliation with practical guidance for relationships and daily
conduct.

### Philippians

Read Philippians for an encouraging letter about joy, humility,
perseverance, and unity even in hardship. It presents Jesus' self-giving
attitude as a model for how to live and serve.

### Colossians

Read Colossians to consider the central place and supremacy of Christ in
creation and the life of the church. It encourages readers to resist
misleading ideas and let their new identity shape their conduct.

### 1 Thessalonians

Read 1 Thessalonians for an encouraging message to a young church facing
pressure and uncertainty. It offers guidance on faith, love, hope, holy
living, and the return of Jesus.

### 2 Thessalonians

Read 2 Thessalonians for guidance about perseverance, the day of the
Lord, and responsible living. It encourages believers not to be
unsettled by speculation or to neglect their everyday responsibilities.

### 1 Timothy

Read 1 Timothy for practical guidance on leadership, teaching, worship,
and community life. It explores how a church can protect sound teaching
and develop trustworthy leaders.

### 2 Timothy

Read 2 Timothy as a personal charge to remain faithful through hardship
and pass on the message of Jesus. It encourages courage, endurance, and
confidence in Scripture.

### Titus

Read Titus for concise guidance on appointing leaders and building
communities marked by integrity and good works. It connects sound
teaching with practical behaviour that benefits others.

### Philemon

Read Philemon for a brief but powerful appeal for reconciliation between
a slaveholder and an enslaved man who has become a Christian. It invites
readers to consider how faith should transform relationships, status,
and the use of power.

### Hebrews

Read Hebrews to explore how Jesus fulfils and surpasses the roles of
priests, sacrifices, and the old covenant. It encourages perseverance by
showing why trust in Jesus is worth holding onto.

### James

Read James for practical teaching about putting faith into action
through speech, patience, humility, and care for people in need. It
challenges readers to examine whether their beliefs are visible in the
way they live.

### 1 Peter

Read 1 Peter for encouragement to remain hopeful and live faithfully
while facing hostility or suffering. It offers a vision of Christian
identity grounded in hope, holiness, and resilient love.

### 2 Peter

Read 2 Peter for warnings about false teachers and reminders to grow in
knowledge and character. It urges readers to remain alert, live
responsibly, and hold onto the promise of Christ's return.

### 1 John

Read 1 John to explore the connection between belief in Jesus, love for
others, and a life shaped by truth. It offers reassurance while
challenging readers to make their faith visible through love and
integrity.

### 2 John

Read 2 John for a short reminder to hold together truth and love while
remaining discerning about misleading teaching. It shows that genuine
care does not require abandoning good judgment.

### 3 John

Read 3 John to see how hospitality, cooperation, and humility support a
healthy Christian community. Its contrast between generous and
controlling leadership offers a useful lesson about how people treat one
another.

### Jude

Read Jude for a forceful warning about people who distort the faith for
their own ends. It encourages readers to remain grounded in what they
have received and to care for others with both mercy and discernment.

### Revelation

Read Revelation for a symbolic vision of evil, endurance, judgment, and
the ultimate renewal of creation. Its central message is one of hope:
oppressive powers do not have the final word, and God's kingdom will
prevail.
